# Ragdoll Couriers art

Every model is generated by a script (`build.py`), so any of them can be tweaked and rebuilt in seconds.
`previews/_vehicles.png`, `previews/_parcels.png`, `previews/_world_1.png` and `previews/_world_2.png` show what's here.

World pieces sit on the ground (bottom at y = 0) and face +Y in Blender, which is the street side
(Roblox -Z). Houses, shops, containers, the depot and the traffic car have a `Paint` part to recolour,
just like the vehicles. For buildings, set `CollisionFidelity` to `Box` (cheap, and riders bounce off cleanly);
for props you can knock over (`physics: true` in the JSON), leave it as `Default` and unanchor them.

## Import into Roblox Studio
1. **Avatar → Import 3D** (or File → Import 3D) and pick an `.fbx` from `export/`.
2. In the import window set **Scale unit: Stud**. Check the size against `size_studs` in the matching `.json`
   (the scooter is about 6.3 studs long). Leave **Import as a single mesh** off, so each part
   (`Body`, `Paint`, `FrontWheel`...) arrives as its own MeshPart.
3. Give each MeshPart a **SurfaceAppearance** using the three textures in `export/`:
   `palette_color.png` → ColorMap, `palette_metal.png` → MetalnessMap, `palette_rough.png` → RoughnessMap.
   Set **AlphaMode: Transparency** on parcels with glass or water (TV, Fish Tank, Hot Tub).
   Upload the three textures once and reuse them for every asset.
4. **Vehicle `Paint` parts are the skin.** Leave them without a SurfaceAppearance and set their `Color` and
   `Material` (e.g. Neon or Glass for rare skins). One vehicle mesh gives unlimited skins.

## What the .json files are for
Each asset has an `export/<name>.json` that the game code will read. All positions are in Roblox
vehicle space (studs; x right, y up, -z forward, relative to the vehicle's physics body):
- `parts.<name>.centre`: where each MeshPart sits
- `seat`, `parcelAnchors`: where the rider sits and parcels ride (the Van holds 4)
- `wheels`: hub centre and radius, for spinning wheels
- `rayPoints`: the four suspension ray origins
- `boxInnerSize`, `sizeClass`, `capacity`: what fits in the cargo box
- `triangles`: per-part counts (budget: 10k per part, checked on every build)

## Rebuild
Needs Blender's Python module (Python 3.13):
```sh
pip install bpy==5.3.0 pillow
python build.py                 # everything (about 90 s on a laptop CPU)
python build.py Parcel_Cake     # just one
```
All colours live in the `PALETTE` table in `lib.py`.

## The city map (batch 3)
`map/plan.py` designs the whole city on a 40-stud grid (about 2,000 × 2,000 studs): the road network,
district plots, buildings, props, hazards and delivery addresses. `map/test_plan.py` checks that every
road connects, tiles join up correctly, nothing sits on a road and every address is reachable.

```sh
python3 map/test_plan.py         # plain Python, no Blender needed
python map/build_map.py          # builds map/city.blend, renders previews/map_*.png, writes src/map/Layout.luau
```

**Open the city in Blender:** `map/city.blend`. Each piece is a linked instance of the kit (the `Kit`
collection), so editing one house edits every copy. Paint colours come from each instance's object colour.

**Terrain (batch 4):** `map/terrain.py` shapes the land: the city stays flat, with rolling hills in the
outskirts, a snow-capped mountain in the north-east, a ring of backdrop hills past the edge, a coast with a
harbour wall at the docks and beaches elsewhere, and a lake in the south-east. It's exported as four
files, `Terrain_NW/NE/SW/SE.fbx`, each holding 36 chunks of 250 × 250 studs (about 90k triangles for the
whole map, with flat areas merged). Import each one as a Model named after its file.

**Road kit (batch 5):** `map/roads.py` lays roads along smooth curves: the **mountain switchbacks**
(five legs and four hairpins, never steeper than 16%, an avalanche shelter, summit car park), the
**lake causeway** (a low curving bridge with railings and lamps) and a **roundabout** with a golden-courier
island. The terrain cuts and fills itself to meet each road, except under bridges. They export as
`Road_Mountain.fbx`, `Road_Causeway.fbx` and `Road_Roundabout.fbx` (import each as a Model). Grid corners
are now rounded (`Road_Corner`).

**Put it in Studio:**
1. Import the models you have into **ServerStorage → CityAssets**, one Model per asset, named like its FBX
   (`House_A`, `Road_Straight`, ...). You don't need all of them: anything missing is drawn as a block of the
   right size and colour.
2. In edit mode (not Play), run in the command bar:
   `require(game.ServerStorage.Map.MapBuilder).build(Vector3.new(3000, 0, 0))`
   The offset keeps the city clear of the kill test road at the origin. Once the game code moves to the
   city, build it with no argument.
3. Save the place. Re-run after importing more models or changing the plan; it replaces `workspace.City`.

## Sky, signs and effects (batch 8)
```sh
python textures/build_textures.py   # Pillow only: 39 decals + 9 particle textures -> export/textures/
python sky/build_sky.py             # Blender: day and sunset skyboxes -> export/sky/<mood>/Skybox*.png
```
- **Decals:** depot, garage and lodge signs, 8 shop signs, district signs, 6 billboard ads, posters,
  road arrows and SLOW, graffiti, and parcel crack decals (3 strengths). Text is Fredoka One
  (`fonts/`, OFL licence), the same font Roblox uses for the UI.
- **Particles:** dust, smoke, spark, crash star, confetti, coin, water splash, speed line, shockwave. White on
  transparent, so a ParticleEmitter's Color tints them (the coin is gold already).
- **Skies:** 1024 px faces. `previews/_sky_*.png` shows them unfolded. If the top seam looks wrong in
  Studio, rotate `SkyboxUp` in 90° steps.

**Hooking them up:** upload `export/textures` and `export/sky` in Studio (Asset Manager, bulk import), paste each
ID into `src/map/DecalIds.luau` (written once by `build_map.py`, never overwritten), then rebuild the city:
`require(game.ServerStorage.Map.MapBuilder).build(Vector3.new(3000, 0, 0), "day")`. Signs, shop fronts,
billboards and the leaderboard header pick up their images, and the sky is set (`"sunset"` for the other one).
Anything without an ID yet is skipped.

## Icons, logo and thumbnails (batch 9)

Run in this order (each needs Blender's Python module and Pillow):

1. `python marketing/build_logo.py`: renders the 3D lettering to `export/marketing/`. That's `logo.png`, `tagline_*.png` and the badge pills `pill_*.png`, all transparent.
2. `python marketing/build_icons.py`: renders 44 store and UI icons to `export/icons/` (512 × 512, transparent). Covers every vehicle, parcel, upgrade, game pass and developer product, with the 3D badge pills where needed. Contact sheet: `previews/_icons.png`.
3. `python marketing/build_thumbnails.py [names...] [--draft]`: needs `map/city.blend`. Renders, staged in the real city:
   - 8 thumbnails (1920 × 1080);
   - `_title` variants carrying the logo and tagline;
   - `loading_screen.png`.
   Contact sheet: `previews/_thumbnails.png`.

4. `python marketing/build_poster_icon.py`: the game icon, `export/marketing/icon.png` (1024). It's front-page advert style: a big courier in Roblox's own Pal Hair (`third_party/roblox`) with a painted screaming face, ink outlines, a flying cake and the scooter, on a starburst. `previews/_icon_check.png` shows it at the 150 and 90 px sizes players actually see.

Upload notes:
- Game icon: `icon.png`.
- Thumbnails: upload several and turn on thumbnail personalisation, so Roblox tests which ones get played. The `_title` versions are the ones with the name on them.
- Game pass and product images: `export/icons/pass_*` and `export/icons/product_*`.
- Loading screen: use it as an ImageLabel in a ScreenGui (`ReplicatedFirst`).

Style follows what top Roblox thumbnails share:
- one big courier with a readable shocked face;
- saturated colour grading with a soft vignette;
- chunky bevelled lettering with a dark outline;
- a short hook line.

## Performance (batch 10)

- `python map/budget.py` counts placed triangles and parts per district into `BUDGET.md`. It fails if a district goes over 3.5M triangles or the city goes over 12,000 parts. Run it after `map/build_map.py`.
- **Triangles: 12.7M down to 7.1M, with no visible change.** Before/after renders were checked zoomed in.
  - Bevels of 0.1 studs or less are now single chamfers.
  - Container ribs are flat.
  - Traffic tyres have 16 sides.
  - Window-box flowers use lighter spheres.
- **MapBuilder physics:** static props have CanTouch off (nothing on the map uses Touched).
  - Props up to 6 studs collide as boxes, and props under 3 studs cast no shadow.
  - Birds have no collision.
  - Terrain and free roads keep precise collision. Road tiles, ramps and buildings keep Default.
  - Everything you can drive on stays queryable, since the suspension raycasts need it.
- **Streaming is on** (`default.project.json`), with target radius 1024 and minimum radius 256 so fast riders never reach unloaded road. If Rojo live sync refuses to set these, set them once on Workspace in Studio.

## Pedestrians

- **Models:** `assets/pedestrians.py` holds eight locals: Shopper, Business, Elder, Kid, Jogger, Tourist, Worker and Student. Each is six MeshParts (Torso, Head, LArm, RArm, LLeg, RLeg) with colours baked in, about 900–1,500 triangles. Preview: `previews/_pedestrians.png`.
- **Walks:** `python map/walks.py` writes `src/shared/Walks.luau`, 485 pavement walks.
  - Walks weave past lamp posts and tree trunks, and stop short of benches, bins and bus stops.
  - Old Town, Downtown and the Hub are three times busier than the Docks; the Suburbs are 1.5 times.
  - `map/test_plan.py` checks every walk point keeps the walker's feet on the pavement and their body clear of every placed item.
- **Game:** `src/client/Pedestrians.client.luau` keeps about 36 walkers around each player. It runs on the client only, with no Humanoids and no network traffic.
  - Walkers swing their limbs, turn round at the ends of their walk, and stop to look around now and then.
  - Riding into one at speed ragdolls them and sends them flying. They fade out after six seconds and are recycled.
- **Import:** bring `export/Ped_*.fbx` into Studio as Models in `ReplicatedStorage.PedAssets`, keeping the part names. Until then, block people with random colours stand in, so it all works straight away. The city must be built first (MapBuilder sets the `Origin` the walks are placed from).

## Pedestrians in the game

With the city built, the server runs in city mode (it falls back to the test road if there's no `workspace.City`):
- Riders spawn at the Hub depot. Jobs go to real addresses 250–900 studs away, with labels such as "Warehouse, Docks".
- **Clip a pedestrian** at speed: they ragdoll, your parcel loses 10% and you lose the no-crash bonus.
- **Near miss:** brushing past someone at 25+ studs/s within 8 studs, without touching, pays 4 coins. At most 6 per job, rate-limited, and the server checks your speed.
- **Customers on foot:** every 4th job goes to a person walking a pavement. Your drop circle walks with them, and handing it over in person pays +30%. Every player sees the same customer.
- **Thieves:** a crash that throws your parcel has a 35% chance of a thief grabbing it. They run along the pavements with it over their head, crossing at junctions. Catch them within 30 seconds to get the parcel back plus 15 coins, otherwise the job fails.

All the numbers are in `src/shared/Config.luau` under "Pedestrians". Customers and thieves move by a shared formula (`src/shared/WalkMath.luau`): a path, a speed and a server-clock start time. Every client draws them in the same place, and the server checks hand-overs and catches against the same maths. Test: `lune run tests/walkmath.spec.luau`.
