# Roblox Concept Tournament (v2, audited)

*28 Nov 2026. This replaces v1, which scored competition from assumptions instead of evidence.*

## What the audit found wrong in v1

1. **Competition was guessed for every concept.** I've now searched Roblox for competitors to all 16; the evidence is listed under each pitch. 13 of 16 scores dropped. The worst errors:
   - **Flip It:** 9 → 5. Yard Sale, Storage Hunters: Open World and Fix It Up! already exist.
   - **Coaster Chaos:** 8 → 4. Theme Park Tycoon 2 is one of Roblox's longest-running hits.
   - **Supermarket Prop Hunt:** 7 → 4. There are many Roblox prop hunt games.
   - **Night Bus:** 7 → 4. There are many Exit 8-style anomaly games on Roblox.
   - **+1 Jump Tower:** 5 → 2. There are dozens of near-identical games.
2. **Some scores contradicted my own match arguments.**
   - I said Pier After Dark was "too close to Sell Fries!" while scoring its competition 8/10.
   - I said Night Bus faced existing anomaly games while scoring it 7/10.
   - Both are fixed.
3. **I used a deciding test in some matches and ignored it in others.** "Is it fun when only 1–3 people are online?" knocked Prop Hunt out of the v1 final, but I never applied it to Sunday League, Getaway Duo or Kitchen Panic. v2 makes it an explicit test used in every match, with no exceptions. That's why Prop Hunt, the 2nd seed, goes out in round one.
4. **One claim was overstated.** I called Sunday League's ball physics "the hardest thing on this list". Island Wars and Coaster Chaos score lower on buildability, so it isn't. Corrected.
5. **Out-of-date fact.** v1 assumed new games are stuck at 16+ without a big budget. Under-16s now open at 100 highly engaged players (you confirmed this), which makes kids' games more viable.

## Assumptions
- **Who's building it:** Ellis, solo, around a full-time job (8–12 hours a week), with Claude writing most of the code through Studio's MCP connection.
- **Budget:** a small ad budget. No 100k-Robux fast-track fee.
- **Goal:** money first.

## What's working right now (Nov 2026)
- On 18 Nov, 30 of the 99 games on Top Playing Now were created in October or November. *(GGRecon)*
- Trends among new games *(GGRecon)*:
  - ragdoll physics (12 of 127 new launches)
  - steal-and-earn egg games
  - incremental "+1" games
  - cozy games with a horror twist
  - vehicles and sports
  - co-op with friends
- Roblox is backing novel games through its Incubator and Jumpstart programmes.
- Roblox's ranking weighs players returning over 28 days, first-minute bounce and playing with friends. *(Roblox Discovery docs)*

## Scoring (unchanged weights)
| Criterion | Weight | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 15% | Can you get it in one sentence and one thumbnail? |
| Loop | 15% | Is the core action fun in a blank room? |
| Retention | 15% | Is there a reason to return on days 8–28? |
| Money | 15% | Are there natural developer products, and does it appeal to spenders? |
| Buildable | 15% | Can one part-timer with Claude ship a good first version in about 4 weeks? |
| Competition | 15% | **Now evidence-based.** 10 = no comparable Roblox game found |
| Social | 10% | Do friends make it better? |

## Knockout tests (new: applied in every match)
- **K1, Shippable:** a buildability score of 3 or less can't ship part-time. It loses to any concept that can.
- **K2, Works almost empty:** is it fun with only 1–3 people online? Every new game spends its first weeks there. A concept that needs a full server loses to one that doesn't, unless the other concept also fails a knockout.

# The 16 pitches, with competition evidence

Scores are listed as Hook / Loop / Retention / Money / Buildable / Competition / Social.

### Ragdoll Couriers: 8/8/6/6/6/**7**/8 → **6.95**
**Concept:** Deliver fragile parcels across a chaotic city on wobbly scooters and vans. You're paid by the parcel's condition. Upgrade vehicles and padding, then unlock harder districts.

**Competition evidence:**
- No ragdoll delivery game found on Roblox. The closest is Delivery Rush, a small beta.
- The idea is proven outside Roblox: Totally Reliable Delivery Service is a ragdoll courier game.
- Ragdoll is the hottest trend right now (12 of 127 new launches), but nobody owns delivery.

**Passes K1** (physics tuning is the risk) **and K2** (fun solo).

### Supermarket Prop Hunt: 8/8/6/7/7/**4**/9 → **6.90**
**Concept:** Hide as a product in a supermarket; security has 3 minutes to find you.

**Competition evidence:** many Roblox prop hunts already exist: Flow's Prop Hunt, PropBlox, Hide and Sneak, Gmod Prop Hunt, a restaurant-map disguise game and others.

**Fails K2:** hide-and-seek needs a full server.

### Flip It (Garage Sale Flip): 7/7/7/8/7/**5**/6 → **6.75**

# Round of 16

**(1) Ragdoll Couriers vs (16) Sumo Animals → Couriers**
Sumo is entering a market that Cat Battles already owns. Couriers is entering an open one. Both are ragdoll games; only one has room to grow.

**(8) Sunday League vs (9) Getaway Duo → Sunday League**
Both fail K2, so the knockout cancels out and it comes down to scores and evidence. Ragdoll Soccer reaching 51k players in a month proves people want ragdoll football. Nothing comparable proves demand for a strict two-player heist, and Getaway's weak monetization (5) would cap it even if it worked. Sunday League goes through, but it fails K2 against anything in the next round.

**(4) Island Wars vs (13) Kitchen Panic → Kitchen Panic (upset by K1)**
Island Wars has the best retention and friend play in the field, but a buildability score of 2 can't ship part-time. Kitchen Panic is mediocre, but buildable.

**(5) Night Bus vs (12) +1 Jump Tower → Night Bus**
+1 Jump Tower has the worst competition evidence of all 16: dozens of games share its exact title. Night Bus also faces anomaly games, but its bus setting and best-in-field hook (9) give it a real chance of being clicked.

**(3) Flip It vs (14) Monster Ranch → Flip It**
Monster Ranch is huge in scope, in a genre whose leader collapsed from 68k to 3k players. Flip It is simpler, has a proven niche and earns better.

**(6) Dig vs (11) Escape the Avalanche → Dig**
Both are in crowded genres. When ready-made templates for a genre are on sale (Escape Tsunami on BuiltByBit), the flood of copies is effectively unlimited. Dig's market is crowded too, but Drill Digging Simulator's 110M visits show it lasts.

**(7) Pier After Dark vs (10) Steal a Pet Rock → Pier**
The clone scores 2 on competition. Pier overlaps with Sell Fries!, but it's still different enough to be clicked.

**(2) Prop Hunt vs (15) Coaster Chaos → Coaster Chaos (upset by K2)**
On scores, Prop Hunt is far better (6.90 vs 5.95), and Coaster Chaos has serious problems: it's up against Theme Park Tycoon 2, and track-building on a phone is a UX nightmare. But Prop Hunt fails K2 and Coaster doesn't. A new prop hunt game with 3 players online has no rounds, so players leave in under a minute, and Roblox stops testing it. Coaster at least works for one player. The rule applies in every match, so Coaster goes through. It's the weakest quarter-finalist.

# Quarter-finals

**QF1: Ragdoll Couriers (1) vs Sunday League (8) → Couriers**
Same trend, and Couriers wins on every remaining point:
- **K2:** a courier run is fun on your own; a football match isn't.
- **Competition:** Sunday League would go straight at Ragdoll Soccer (51k players). Couriers has no Roblox rival.
- **Build:** networked ball physics plus ragdolls lag worse than vehicles plus ragdolls.

**QF2: Kitchen Panic (13) vs Night Bus (5) → Night Bus**
- Night Bus wins on hook (9 vs 7) and buildability (8 vs 5). They're level on competition (4 each).
- Kitchen's solo play is weak (borderline K2). Night Bus anomaly runs are already designed to work solo.
- Night Bus's retention problem (5) is real, and Kitchen's is no better (5).

**QF3: Flip It (3) vs Dig to the Core (6) → Flip It**
- Both pass both knockouts. Flip It's competition is 5 to Dig's 3: there are a few direct rivals, versus dozens of digging games.
- Retention and money are equal (7 and 8 each).
- Flip It's inspection mini-game gives it skill and mastery that a click-to-dig loop lacks.

**QF4: Pier After Dark (7) vs Coaster Chaos (15) → Pier**
Both pass both knockouts, so it's on merit. Pier scores 6.45 to Coaster's 5.95. Pier overlaps with Sell Fries!, but Coaster has to compete with Theme Park Tycoon 2, which is the bigger problem. Pier is also easier to build: two systems, but no track editor on a phone.

# Final: Ragdoll Couriers vs Flip It

| | Ragdoll Couriers | Flip It |
|---|---|---|
| Score | **6.95** | 6.75 |
| K1 / K2 | ✅ / ✅ | ✅ / ✅ |
| Hook | **8** | 7 |
| Loop | **8** | 7 |
| Retention | 6 | **7** |
| Money | 6 | **8** |
| Buildable | 6 | **7** |
| Competition | **7**: no Roblox rival found | 5: Yard Sale, Storage Hunters, Fix It Up! |
| Social | **8** | 6 |
| Trend fit | Ragdoll is the #1 new-game trend | Proven but steady genre |
| Free marketing | Every crash is a funny clip | Rare finds are mildly shareable |

**Case for Flip It:** more money and longer retention, which is what Roblox's 28-day ranking rewards. It's also lower risk to build.

**Case for Couriers:** Roblox's ranking starts with play-through rate, the share of people who click your game after seeing it. You don't get to prove retention until people click. Couriers has the better hook, rides the platform's hottest trend, and has no direct rival. Flip It would launch next to Storage Hunters, which already does the "is it treasure?" fantasy, has events and has a head start. Couriers' weaker money (6) is fixable with design: vehicles, padding consumables and troll products are proven sellers. Its competition can't be designed away.

**The decisive risk:** Couriers' one real danger is physics. If a ragdoll scooter feels janky on a phone, players bounce, and that is fatal.

## 🏆 Champion: Ragdoll Couriers, with a one-week kill test

**Week 1 kill test:** build only a scooter, a ragdoll crash and one parcel on a test street, then play it on your phone. Pass if crashing is funny and controlling the scooter feels good within 2 minutes of play. **If it fails, switch to Flip It**, the runner-up, which has no equivalent risk.

That gives you the higher-upside concept, with a cheap test before committing and a strong fallback.

# The champion's outline: Ragdoll Couriers

**Vision line:** "Deliver anything, anywhere, in one piece (probably)."

**First version, about 4 weeks after the kill test:**
1. One small city district: houses, a hill, a market, a building site.
2. Scooter first; van and cargo bike later.
3. A job board: parcels range from pillows (easy) to TVs, fish tanks and wedding cakes (fragile).
4. Pay = base × condition %, plus a speed bonus. Ragdoll crashes damage the parcel.
5. Upgrades: suspension, padding, a faster scooter, a bigger cargo box.
6. Districts unlock as you level: suburbs → old town (cobbles) → docks → mountain road.
7. Co-op jobs: a sofa needs two players, with each player holding one end.

**Products:**
- **Passes:** 2x Pay, VIP vehicle, Bubble-Wrap Pro (permanent extra padding).
- **Developer products:** cash packs, a rocket boost, a "Parcel Insurance" consumable, and troll products (drop a banana peel, spawn a traffic jam). In Yann's games, troll products made 80k Robux.

**Retention:**
- Daily routes.
- 3 of 10 daily challenges ("deliver a fish tank without a scratch").
- Vehicle collection.
- Weekly leaderboards for "cleanest courier".
- A week-2 update planned before launch.

**Launch:**
- Thumbnail: a courier flying with a TV. The first minute must deliver that (a crash within 30 seconds).
- A gameplay video on the game page.
- Post the funniest crashes as Shorts during development.
- Small ads to reach 100 highly engaged players and unlock under-16s.
